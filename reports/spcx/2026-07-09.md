## 4. Lockup status — the dominant forward risk, unchanged in structure, changed in odds
- **No Form 4 or Form 144 filings found** for SpaceX insiders in an EDGAR-targeted search this cycle — insiders remain locked, Musk barred from all early-release tranches (unchanged from 6/15).
- **First tranche mechanics unchanged:** 20% of eligible non-Musk locked shares release on the 2nd trading day after Q2 earnings, **plus a conditional +10% bonus if SPCX trades ≥ $175.50 (30% above the $135 IPO price) on 5 of the 10 sessions into earnings.**
  - **This is the single biggest change since 6/15: the bonus trigger was "currently tracking to hit" in the last report (spot ~$178 in mid-June). At today's ~$148–150, SPCX is ~15% below the $175.50 threshold and would need a sharp rally into early August to qualify.** If it doesn't trigger, ~10% less supply hits in the first tranche — modestly bullish for near-term float, but only because the stock already fell.
- **Earnings date itself is unconfirmed and sources disagree: Aug 6 vs. "around Aug 11" vs. generically "late July/early August."** SpaceX has not formally announced a date. Treat Aug 6–11 as the working window; this also is the trigger for FULL-mode escalation on the day it's confirmed.
- Tranche schedule unchanged: 7% each on day 70/90/105/120/135 (~8/21, 9/10, 9/25, 10/10, 10/25); 28% after Q3 earnings (late Oct/early Nov); remainder on the 12/8 180-day cliff; Musk + select investors 6/12/2027.
- **New qualitative risk:** former SEC Chair Gary Gensler warned publicly of a "great rebalancing" — VCs and sovereign wealth funds trimming a third to three-quarters of their SpaceX exposure once lockups open, distinct from and additive to the mechanical tranche schedule. Separately, a former Nasdaq CEO warned the "unprecedented" lockup structure could eventually put on the order of **$800B in share value** into tradeable form (a function of the ~$1.5–2T-plus market cap and ~95%+ of shares still locked, not a claim that all of it sells at once — treat as an upper-bound framing device, not a forecast). Both are commentary/opinion, not new mechanical facts, but they reflect a market narrative shift toward supply anxiety since 6/15.
