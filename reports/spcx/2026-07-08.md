## 3. What moved since Jun 15 (the big gap)
This was an eventful three-and-a-half weeks — five distinct catalysts, mostly negative for price despite constructive fundamentals:

1. **Options launched Jun 16** — record first-day single-stock options volume (~1.8M contracts, ~$2.8B premium).
2. **$60B Cursor acquisition announced Jun 16** (all-stock, expected to close Q3) — stock spiked to its **$225.64 ATH the same day**, then reversed hard as investors questioned the price tag and integration logic; commentary pegs **~$600B of market cap erased over the following days** as the "AI arms race" framing gave way to valuation skepticism.
3. **$25B inaugural bond offering** — announced Jun 22, ~$90B of orders, upsized from $20B, priced across 5 tranches (5.35%–6.65%, 2031–2056 maturities), closed Jun 26. The *announcement* alone reportedly drove a ~12% single-day drop (to $154.60) on capital-intensity/dilution-adjacent concerns, even though it's debt, not equity.
4. **Colossus 2 litigation risk** — an NAACP suit alleges xAI/MZX Tech is running ~27–35 unpermitted methane turbines at the Colossus 2 site; a court shutdown order could jeopardize part of the **$45B Anthropic compute contract** (Anthropic pays $1.25B/mo through May 2029). DOJ has intervened on the "national security" side to keep turbines running; near-term outcome uncertain.
5. **Nasdaq-100 inclusion went effective Jul 7** (confirmed by Nasdaq IR release) — completing the index-inclusion trifecta. Several outlets are now drawing a **Palantir-style "sell-the-news"** analogy (PLTR peaked into its Dec-2024 Nasdaq-100 add, then fell ~23–25% over subsequent weeks).
6. **(Jul 8, overnight/today)** JPMorgan published a note calling a **Tesla–SpaceX merger** idea "strategically coherent on paper" (AI/robotics/energy vertical integration, Musk unifying control) but flagging serious obstacles — Musk holds ~85% of SpaceX voting power vs. only ~20% of Tesla's, plus China-approval and governance/dilution risk for TSLA minority holders. This is analyst speculation, not a confirmed corporate action, but it's actively moving both tickers today (TSLA up on the note; SPCX reaction still forming).

Operationally: **Starlink subscribers reported at 10M**, more than double the ~5.5M figure at IPO — a genuine fundamental positive largely overshadowed by the AI-compute/lockup/valuation narrative.

## 5. Lockup status — the dominant supply overhang, now sharper-dated
- **Confirmed date shift:** first Q2 earnings + first lockup tranche is now dated **August 6, 2026** (not the ~Aug 11 estimate in the prior report) — two trading days after earnings.
- **20% base tranche** unlocks Aug 6.
- **Conditional +10% "bonus" tranche:** unlocks only if SPCX closes ≥$175.50 (30% above the $135 IPO price) on **5 of the 10 trading days ending on the earnings date**. With the stock currently $150–159, **this trigger is not on track** — it touched $225.64 once (Jun 16) but has spent essentially no time near $175.50 in the run-up window that will matter (the 10 sessions into Aug 6). Barring a sharp rally, expect the base 20% only, not 30%.
- Days to next tranche: **~29** (Jul 8 → Aug 6). Days to the **Dec 8, 2026 cliff** (remainder, non-Musk): **~153**.
- **Sentiment on the overhang:** mixed. Coatue (large holder) told CNBC it's "not rushing to cash out" — post-lockup selling becomes "a portfolio decision" based on SpaceX's weight in the fund. Conversely, former SEC Chair Gary Gensler warned of a possible **"great rebalancing"** — VCs/SWFs could trim a third-to-three-quarters of concentrated SpaceX exposure once free to sell, which would be a materially larger overhang than a base-case orderly unlock.
- Full schedule (unchanged in structure from prior report): 7% tranches Aug 21/Sep 10/Sep 25/Oct 10/Oct 25 → 28% at Q3 earnings (late Oct/early Nov) → remainder Dec 8 → Musk + select investors Jun 12, 2027.
