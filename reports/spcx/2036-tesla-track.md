# SPCX in 2036 — "Assume SpaceX Tracks Like Tesla"

**Prepared:** 2026-07-15 · **Question:** normalizing for float and IPO valuation, if SPCX tracks TSLA post-IPO, what is a share worth in 2036?
**Anchor mapping:** SPCX IPO 2026-06-12 ↔ TSLA IPO 2010-06-29, so calendar 2036 = Tesla's calendar 2020 (IPO +9.5 to +10.5 years).
All inputs below were independently fact-checked (24/24 load-bearing claims confirmed against SEC filings, Tesla IR, CNBC/Bloomberg and market data).

## TL;DR — the answer is a ladder, and it depends what "tracks like Tesla" means

| Interpretation | SPCX/share in 2036 | Implied market cap | Your 2,480 sh |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Literal price track** (same % return off IPO price) — mid-2036 | **~$8,000** | $105T | $19.9M |
| **A. Literal price track** — end-2036 (Tesla's 2020 blow-off) | **~$28,000** | $369T | $69.5M |
| **D. Scale-honest "Tesla in spirit"** (best mega-cap run ever recorded) | **~$750–1,350** | $10–18T | $1.9–3.3M |
| **C. What Tesla actually did once it was already worth $1T** | **~$185** | $2.4T | $0.46M |

The literal answer is arithmetic fantasy: $105T is **71% of the value of every listed company on Earth today** ($147.6T), and $369T is 2.5x all of it. The defensible reading of "tracks like Tesla, normalized for starting valuation" is the $750–1,350 band; the sober stage-matched comp says ~$185. For reference, the analyst consensus target one month post-IPO is $210 — the street is already pricing SPCX as a mature mega-cap, not as 2010 Tesla.

Float noise also conveniently cancels right now: after spiking to $225.64 (Jun 16) on ~4.3%-float index-inclusion mechanics, SPCX round-tripped to close at **$136.08 on July 14 — back at the IPO price**, so anchoring to $135 vs. the current price changes nothing. (Tesla, for what it's worth, also broke below its IPO price within two weeks — $14.98 in July 2010.)

## 2. Scenario A — the literal track (what the question asks)

Tesla's split-adjusted price multiples vs. its $17 IPO, applied to SPCX's $135:

| Tesla date | Multiple | SPCX equivalent date | SPCX/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 2019 (9-yr trough, $178.97) | 10.5x | mid-2035 | $1,421 |
| Dec 31, 2019 ($418.33) | 24.6x | end-2035 | $3,322 |
| Mar 2020 (covid crash, $524) | 30.8x | Mar 2036 | $4,161 |
| **Jun 29, 2020 (10-yr mark, $1,009.35)** | **59.4x** | **mid-2036** | **$8,015** |
| Sep 2020 ($2,145 pre-split terms) | 126.2x | Sep 2036 | $17,034 |
| **Dec 31, 2020 ($3,528.35 pre-split terms)** | **207.6x** | **end-2036** | **$28,019** |

Two things to notice:

1. **"In 2036" spans an 8x range.** Tesla's entire legend is backloaded: it was up only 10.5x nine years in, 24.6x at year 9.5, and then went up **+743% during calendar 2020 alone**. On the literal track, SPCX enters 2036 around $3,300 and exits at $28,000. Any single number for "2036" is really a bet on which month.
2. **The full year-by-year path implies a boring decade first** (Tesla year-end closes → SPCX equivalents): end-2026 $211 · 2027 $227 · 2028 $269 · 2029 $1,195 (Tesla's 2013 Model-S-ramp breakout, +344%) · 2030 $1,766 · 2031 $1,906 · 2032 $1,697 · 2033 $2,472 · 2034 $2,643 · 2035 $3,322 · 2036 $28,019. "Tracking like Tesla" means roughly **two years of dead money below $270** while lockups clear (Aug–Dec 2026 tranches; Musk June 2027), then a Starship/Starlink-margin catalyst circa 2029 playing the role of the Model S.

**Why the literal track cannot happen:** at $8,015/share SPCX would be worth $105T on today's share count — 71% of all world equity ($147.6T) and ~36% of 2036 world equity even if the whole pond compounds 7%/yr to ~$290T. At $28,019 it's $369T, 2.5x every listed company on Earth combined, and with Tesla-like dilution $759T. The largest single-company share of world equity ever recorded is Nvidia at ~5% of MSCI ACWI (~3.4% of all listed equity, Oct 2025). Tesla's decade multiplied a **$1.6B** micro-cap ~300x in market-cap terms; SpaceX starts **~1,100x bigger**. Percentage returns are not scale-invariant — that is precisely what "normalizing for IPO valuation" has to mean.

The fundamental version is equally impossible: Tesla grew revenue ~270x ($116.7M → $31.5B) over its first decade. SpaceX repeating that from $18.7B means **$5.0T of revenue in 2036 — 4.3% of world GDP** (largest revenue ever recorded: Walmart, ~$0.68T).

## 3. Scenario C — what Tesla actually did at SpaceX's current scale

SpaceX at IPO ($1.77–1.79T) is *already bigger than Tesla is today* (~$1.5T, $396.18 on Jul 14, 2026) and bigger than Tesla's all-time-peak cap (~$1.54T, Dec 2024). So the stage-matched question is: how did Tesla compound *after* it first became a trillion-dollar company (Oct 25, 2021, $341.62 split-adj)?

Answer: **+16% total in 4.7 years ≈ 3.2%/yr** — despite revenue tripling — because its price/sales compressed from ~21–26x to ~15x. Applying 3.2%/yr to SPCX for 10 years: **~$185/share (~$2.4T cap) in 2036**. Multiple compression eating fundamental growth is the normal fate of a company that IPOs at 95x sales; this is the scenario the July 2026 analyst consensus ($210 avg PT; Goldman $205, Morgan Stanley $300, Raymond James $800 outlier) implicitly lives in.

## 4. Scenario D — the scale-honest ceiling ("best decade any mega-cap ever had")

If "tracks like Tesla" means "becomes its era's defining growth stock," the right template is the best runs ever achieved from a large base — and none of them look remotely like 59–207x:

| Precedent | Run | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft (Nadella era) | $300B (2014) → $3T (2024) | **10x in 10y** — the mega-cap record |
| Apple | $500B (2016) → $4T (2025) | ~8x in 9.5y |
| Nvidia (the outlier of all time) | $1T (May 2023) → $5.5T (May 2026) | **5.5x in 3y — the largest multiple ever achieved from a $1T+ base** |

No company has ever 10x'd from above $1T. The ceilings:

- **Concentration-record ceiling:** SPCX matches Nvidia's all-time ~3.4% of world equity in a 2036 pond of ~$290T → **~$10T cap → ~$750/share** (18.7%/yr).
- **Generous 5%-of-ACWI ceiling:** ~$12T → **~$940/share** (21.4%/yr).
- **Microsoft-record 10x:** $17.7T → **~$1,345/share** (25.8%/yr) — this would already make SpaceX history's best-ever performer from a $1T+ start by roughly 2x.

**Fundamentals cross-check** (2030 street revenue forecasts grown 20%/yr to 2036, at mature-mega-cap multiples): Morgan Stanley's $330B-by-2030 path → $600–1,120/share (8–15x P/S); Goldman's $474B path → $860–1,610. Musk's "$1T revenue by 2030-31" claim at 8–15x would blow through all of it — and Tesla's history is the cautionary tale on Musk revenue timelines. The fundamental band converges on the same **$600–1,600** region as the concentration math.

## 5. Bottom line

- **Literal answer to the question as posed:** ~**$8,000** at the 10-year mark (mid-2036), ~**$28,000** if it replays Tesla's 2020 year-end mania — arithmetically what "tracks like Tesla, normalized for float and IPO valuation" produces, and economically impossible (it prices one company at 1–2.5x all listed equity on Earth).
- **Scale-honest "Tesla of its decade" answer:** **~$750–1,350**, i.e. a $10–18T company, requiring SpaceX to beat the best 10-year mega-cap run ever recorded (19–26%/yr for a decade).
- **Base case if it merely does what Tesla itself did from this market cap:** **~$185** — roughly where it trades today plus ~3%/yr, which is also roughly what the street's $210 consensus target says.
